# A Comprehensive Survey on World Models in Artificial Intelligence: From Cognitive Foundations to Modern Neural Simulators and LLM Planning

## TL;DR
- World models serve as internal generative representations that enable intelligent systems to simulate environmental dynamics, anticipate future outcomes, and plan actions without requiring direct trial-and-error in physical reality [1][2][3].
- Modern neural architectures bridge classical model-based reinforcement learning and large-scale video generation, employing autoregressive transformers and latent diffusion models to capture rich spatial-temporal details [4][5][6].
- Large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models leverage internal world modeling for test-time planning, Monte Carlo tree search, and causal reasoning in interactive environments [7][8][9].
- Evaluating world models reveals a critical disconnect between perceptual video realism and downstream control utility, necessitating decision-making-centric benchmarks and uncertainty-aware modeling [10][11].

## Background
The concept of a world model originates in classical cognitive science, control theory, and early cybernetics, where intelligent agents maintain an internal representation of the external environment to guide adaptive behavior [12][13]. Seminal theoretical frameworks such as Kenneth Craik's mental models, Edward Tolman's cognitive maps, and Helmholtz’s predictive coding established that biological systems act to actively minimize free energy and prediction errors [12][13]. In artificial intelligence, these cognitive principles manifested in foundational reinforcement learning architectures like Richard Sutton's Dyna framework, which integrates learning, planning, and reactive execution by updating an internal model of action effects [2]. 

In modern model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL), a world model approximates the transition dynamics of a Markov Decision Process (MDP), allowing agents to simulate trajectories and optimize policies sample-efficiently [14][3]. A landmark milestone in deep reinforcement learning was the introduction of Recurrent World Models by Ha and Schmidhuber (2018), which demonstrated that compact spatial-temporal representations extracted by unsupervised neural networks could be used to train successful policies entirely within "hallucinated" dream environments before transferring back to the physical task [1]. Over the past decade, the definition of world models has expanded from compact low-dimensional transition models to large-scale generative simulators spanning video, vision, and language modalities [3][15][9].

## Neural World Models and Generative Simulators
Modern neural world models leverage advancements in representation learning, discrete tokenization, and diffusion processes to simulate complex environment dynamics [4][5][6]. A central division in contemporary neural world modeling lies between discrete token-based autoregressive models and continuous latent diffusion simulators [16]. For example, the IRIS architecture demonstrated that combining a discrete image autoencoder with an autoregressive Transformer enables sample-efficient model-based reinforcement learning in Atari environments [6], a design further refined through context-aware tokenization in Delta-IRIS [17]. In parallel, continuous diffusion world models such as DIAMOND established top-tier performance on Atari 100k benchmarks by explicitly preserving fine-grained visual dynamics and intricate details [5]. Probing these latent environment simulators reveals that they develop linearly decodable representations of internal state variables such as object positions and game scores [16].

Scaling beyond low-resolution games, generative video models have emerged as expansive world simulators capable of capturing rich physical interactions from unlabelled data [4][15]. Foundation models such as OpenAI's Sora treat variable-duration video and image spacetime patches with text-conditional diffusion transformers, exhibiting emergent capabilities like 3D consistency, long-range object permanence, and interactive simulation [15]. Similarly, Google DeepMind's Genie operates at 11 billion parameters to provide frame-by-frame user controllability over generated virtual worlds via a latent action space, enabling unsupervised imitation learning from unlabelled internet video clips [4][18]. In robotic manipulation, frameworks like GE-Act 2.0 scale control-oriented autoencoders and visual planners to facilitate scalable zero-shot physical execution [19].

## World Models for LLMs and Agent Planning
Beyond traditional robotics and reinforcement learning, large language models (LLMs) and vision-language-action (VLA) models increasingly function as cognitive world models that perform internal reasoning and search over future states [7][8][9]. While standard chain-of-thought prompting often suffers from planning bottlenecks and lack of environment feedback [20], integrating structured planning algorithms such as Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) with LLMs—exemplified by Reasoning via Planning (RAP)—repurposes language models to simulate environment states and anticipate action outcomes [7]. 

To bridge static language knowledge with dynamic interactive tasks, recent architectures incorporate causal representation learning (CRL) and multi-modal grounding [8][9]. For instance, Causal World Models map disentangled causal variables and actions to and from natural language, allowing agents to explicitly reason about interventions and counterfactual consequences over extended planning horizons [8]. In vision-language domains, frameworks like the Vision Language World Model (VLWM) and Qwen-AgentWorld combine system-1 reactive action decoding with system-2 reflective planning via cost minimization, supporting scalable simulation and agentic reinforcement learning across thousands of diverse environments [21][9]. Unified tokenization models such as OmniJARVIS further integrate multimodal interaction data with behavior encoders and autoregressive transformers to execute open-world instruction following in complex simulation environments like Minecraft [22].

## Evaluation, Open Challenges, and Future Directions
Despite rapid progress in generative video fidelity and model scaling, rigorous evaluation remains a central challenge in world model research [10][11]. A pervasive issue in current literature is the "claim/evidence mismatch," wherein models are frequently evaluated on perceptual metrics (such as Fréchet Video Distance or pixel reconstruction loss) rather than downstream control utility or policy optimization performance [10]. Predictive accuracy in pixel space does not guarantee control utility, as minor visual errors can compound catastrophically during long-horizon rollouts [10][23]. Furthermore, optimized planners frequently exploit model over-optimism—a vulnerability quantified by the "exploitability gap" ($XGap$) where policies leverage model errors rather than robust environment dynamics [10].

In domains such as autonomous driving and physical robotics, deploying world models encounters severe hurdles including distribution shift, compounding errors in offline datasets, and multi-sensor integration challenges [23][24][25]. Imagine-2-Drive and uncertainty-aware offline MBRL frameworks address these limitations by incorporating multi-modal diffusion policies and uncertainty quantification to maintain robustness under partial observability and noisy real-world conditions [23][24]. Emerging benchmarks—such as WorldModelBench for video generation, WorldPrediction for reasoning, and WorldArena for evaluating the perceptual-functional gap—strive to align evaluation metrics with real functional utility [11]. Ultimately, future advancements in world model research depend on developing physically consistent, uncertainty-aware, and decision-making-centric architectures that bridge the divide between high-level reasoning and reliable physical interaction [10][11].
